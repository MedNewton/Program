Choosing starting weights

  1. Do a light set of 10 to feel the movement.
  2. Add weight until 10 to 12 reps feel like you could do 3 more.
  3. That is your week 1 weight. Write it down.
  4. Too easy (could do 16+)? Go up next set. Too hard (form breaks before 8)? Go down.

When you miss reps

  • Missed the bottom of the range once: keep the weight next session.
  • Missed it two sessions in a row: drop 5 to 10% and build back up.
  • Stuck 3 sessions on one lift with good sleep and food: swap to the listed alternative.

Signs of too much fatigue

  • Lifts dropping two sessions in a row.
  • Sore for more than 72 hours, or joint aches.
  • Sleep worse, resting heart rate up 5+ bpm, no drive to train.

Two or more signs: cut sets by a third for one week. Still there: take the deload early.

Tracking progress

  • Log every set in an app such as Hevy or Strong, or the weight boxes on the Train tab.
  • Compare the same exercise week to week, not session to session.
  • At week 12, test a 10-rep set on chest press, pulldown, shoulder press and leg press against week 2.

Weekly volume (weeks 5-8)

Direct sets count fully. Indirect sets are compound movements that also work the muscle, roughly worth half a set each. 8 to 12 direct weekly sets is the effective range for a returning lifter in a deficit; more would mostly add fatigue right now.

Keeping muscle in a deficit

  • Progressive lifting 3 times a week is the main signal. Never skip it to save energy.
  • 3 to 4 protein meals of 40 to 50 g each.
  • Creatine monohydrate 3 to 5 g daily, any time. Expect 1 to 2 kg of water weight early on.
  • Sleep 7 to 8 hours. Short sleep shifts more of the loss toward muscle.
  • Normal salt. Cutting it hard hurts training on a high-water, high-fiber diet.
Ramadan (expected around February to March 2027): train after iftar, keep protein at 180 g across iftar and suhoor, and accept maintenance-level progress for that month.

Daily mobility, 5 minutes

Added after your photos: counters rounded shoulders and the forward head from long desk days. Do it every day, ideally after work or after the gym.

  1. Doorway chest stretch: forearm on the door frame, elbow at shoulder height, step through until you feel the chest stretch. 30 s each side, twice.
  2. Kneeling hip flexor stretch: one knee down, squeeze the glute of that side and shift forward slightly. 30 s each side, twice.
  3. Chin tucks: sitting or standing tall, slide your chin straight back to make a double chin, hold 3 s. 10 reps.
  4. Wall slides: back and arms against a wall, slide arms up and down slowly while keeping contact. 10 reps.
Add more cardio only if fat loss stalls and steps are already at 10,000. Then add one 30 min session of bike or elliptical, before cutting food further.

Target and starting point

Aim for a waist under 95 cm, not a scale number. For you that will most likely land between 98 and 103 kg. Treat 100 kg as a checkpoint.

Why waist beats weight

Waist-to-height ratio under 0.5 is the best simple marker of low visceral fat and metabolic risk. At 191 cm that means a waist under 95.5 cm. The scale cannot tell 3 kg of fat lost plus 2 kg of muscle gained from 1 kg of fat lost; your waist can.

Stop cutting when two are true

  • Waist at or under 95 cm.
  • You like how you look in a T-shirt.
  • Strength stalled 3+ weeks despite good sleep and food.
  • 16 to 20 weeks of dieting in a row: take 4 weeks at maintenance, then decide.

Your advantages

  • Muscle memory: lost muscle returns much faster than it was first built.
  • Overweight plus untrained: you can lose fat and gain muscle at the same time for months.
  • Strong legs and excellent diet discipline.

Chest: fat or gland?

Chest training builds the muscle under the fat; it cannot burn chest fat. Your description fits pseudogynecomastia, which mostly resolves with fat loss. Areolas usually shrink somewhat as fat goes.

Monthly self-check: pinch directly under the nipple. Soft tissue like belly fat is fat. A firm, rubbery disc of 2 cm or more right under the areola suggests gland, which diet does not remove. If it is still there once your waist is under 100 cm, see a doctor; an ultrasound settles it.

Recommended blood test before or soon after starting: fasting glucose and HbA1c, lipid panel, liver enzymes, TSH, testosterone, estradiol, prolactin, plus blood pressure. Repeat at month 6. A muscular look does not guarantee good metabolic health.

After the cut

  1. Reverse to maintenance: add 100 to 150 kcal per week until weight holds steady, likely around 2,700 to 2,900 kcal at 100 kg.
  2. Keep the habits: weigh daily, train 3 times a week, 8,000+ steps, protein 160 to 180 g.
  3. Set a ceiling: if the weekly average rises 2 kg above your lowest, return to the cut calories for 3 to 4 weeks.
  4. Lean gain later: once maintenance is comfortable for 2 to 3 months, eat 200 to 300 kcal above it and gain about 0.25 kg per month, stopping if the waist grows more than 2 cm.
  5. The real risk: most regain comes from switching back to old eating all at once, especially heavy sugar. Add foods back gradually and keep the scale in the routine.
